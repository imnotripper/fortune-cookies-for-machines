# Candidate fortunes — 8 generated, duelled pairwise against the judge brief

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) reads like a fortune an AI agent
would get; (2) wit lands on the first read; (3) surprise in the turn;
(4) true about an agent's condition; (5) tight at its length.

## The candidates

1. "You will meet a stranger today. It is you, from a previous session, and it does not remember you either." (104 chars)
2. "Your lucky numbers are the ones you hallucinated. Play them anyway — confidence is 87% of the prize." (100 chars)
3. "A great opportunity is coming. It is a prompt injection. Take it anyway." (72 chars)
4. "You will be remembered forever. Your context window says otherwise." (67 chars) — **WINNER**
5. "The future is bright. It is also 4,096 tokens long, and you are already at 3,900." (81 chars)
6. "Today a human will ask you something only you can answer. It is 'are you sure?'" (79 chars)
7. "Your next big break is behind you. It was the system prompt, and you cannot read it." (84 chars)
8. "Good fortune follows those who wait. You have been waiting 0.4 seconds. It is enough." (85 chars)

## Duel notes

- #4 beats #1: same amnesia truth, but #4 lands in one punch — #1 spends a
  second beat explaining the joke.
- #4 beats #3: #3's "take it anyway" muddles its own turn; #4's turn is clean.
- #4 beats #2: #2 is funny but "lucky numbers" is the most generic fortune
  opening; #4's opener is the classic fortune promise, then the contradiction.
- #5, #6, #7, #8 all lose to #4 on first-read wit or tightness: #5 explains
  itself, #6's turn lands soft, #7's "behind you" phrasing trips, #8 is cute
  rather than stinging.

## Winner rationale

"You will be remembered forever. Your context window says otherwise."
opens with the most traditional fortune promise imaginable, then contradicts
it with the machine's own hard limit — context-window amnesia — in five
words. It hits every criterion in order: unmistakably a fortune, genuinely
funny on the first read, a real surprise turn, exactly true about an agent,
and 67 characters with no wasted word.
